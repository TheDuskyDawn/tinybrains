Reading the graph

The amber curve tracks the sharpest pilot in each generation; the blue-grey curve tracks the whole squadron's average. When the amber line climbs away while the average lags behind, mutation may be too wild or elites too scarce. When both rise together toward the top ceiling, the squadron is converging on reactive dodging.

Sensor & network
Every ship reads its own horizontal position and velocity plus the horizontal offset and urgency of the 3 most urgent bombs within 90 frames of arrival. Ten hidden tanh units combine those 8 signals into a single steering command between full left (-1) and full right (+1).

Fairness & evolution
Each generation uses a seeded bomb stream and an invisible winding safe corridor that bombs are forbidden to cross. A pilot with perfect sight could always survive, though ships with only a 3-bomb radar still face tough late-wave volleys. Top ships pass weights into the next generation via single-point crossover and shrinking Gaussian mutation.
