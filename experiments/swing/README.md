# Swing

A browser-based neuroevolution simulation where a population of small neural networks learns to pump energy into an underactuated pendulum, swing it up from hanging down, and balance it upright against disturbances.

The simulation runs entirely client-side in vanilla HTML5, CSS, and JavaScript with zero dependencies and no build step.

---

## What It Is

In classical control theory, the inverted pendulum swing-up is a canonical benchmark for underactuated robotics. An actuator is underactuated when it cannot produce arbitrary acceleration in arbitrary directions: here, the motor can exert at most **4.0 N·m** of torque, while holding a 1 kg point mass at horizontal against 9.8 m/s² gravity requires **9.8 N·m**.

A static push can never lift the pendulum directly. To reach the top, the neural networks must learn:
1. **Resonant Pumping:** Apply torque in phase with velocity when swinging near the bottom to inject mechanical energy ($E = \frac{1}{2}\omega^2 + g\cos\theta$).
2. **Phase Reversal / Braking:** Switch or damp torque near the apex so the pendulum does not overshoot or spin wildly.
3. **Upright Balancing (PD Regime):** Regulate fine torque near $\theta = 0$ ($|\theta| < 0.2$ rad, $|\omega| < 1.5$ rad/s) to keep the pendulum standing upright and reject unexpected kicks.

Users watch the entire training process live generation by generation—from chaotic twitching, to rhythmic energy pumping, to swing-up, to robust balance.

## How the Simulation Works

### 1. Population & Physics
- **Population:** 50 pendulums by default sharing a single pivot at the center of the canvas.
- **Physical Model:** Massless rigid rod of length $L = 1.0\text{ m}$ (rendered as 170 px) with a point mass $m = 1.0\text{ kg}$ at the tip ($I = m L^2 = 1.0\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$).
- **Equations of Motion:** Fixed time step $dt = 0.02\text{ s}$ (50 Hz).
  $$\alpha = g \sin(\theta) + \tau - b \omega$$
  $$\omega \leftarrow \text{clamp}(\omega + \alpha \cdot dt, -8, +8)$$
  $$\theta \leftarrow \text{wrap}(\theta + \omega \cdot dt, -\pi, +\pi]$$
  where $\theta = 0$ is upright, $\theta = \pi$ is hanging down, $g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2$, friction $b = 0.05$, and torque $\tau = u \cdot \tau_{\text{max}}$ with output $u \in [-1, 1]$ and $\tau_{\text{max}} = 4.0\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$.

### 2. Fairness & Seeded Disturbance
In every generation, all 50 pendulums face the exact same test conditions generated by a reproducible Mulberry32 PRNG (seed: $\text{generation} \times 7919 + 12345$):
- **Initial Angle:** $\theta_0 = \pi \pm 0.3\text{ rad}$
- **Initial Angular Velocity:** $\omega_0 = 0.0 \pm 0.5\text{ rad/s}$
- **Two Kick Disturbances:**
  - Kick 1: Between 60% and 72% of the episode duration.
  - Kick 2: Between 78% and 92% of the episode duration.
  - Magnitude: $\pm \text{pushSize} \times [0.7, 1.0]\text{ rad/s}$. At the default `pushSize = 0.5 rad/s` (carrying $\approx 0.125\text{ J}$ of kinetic energy), disturbances are solvable and recoverable near the upright position.

### 3. Neural Architecture (61 Weights)
Each pendulum is driven by a compact feed-forward network:
- **4 Inputs:** $\sin(\theta)$, $\cos(\theta)$, $\text{clamp}(\omega / 8, -1, 1)$, and previous motor output $u_{t-1}$ (starts at $0.0$). Using sine and cosine eliminates angular discontinuity across $\pm \pi$.
- **10 Hidden Units:** $\tanh$ activation.
- **1 Output Unit:** $\tanh$ activation representing motor torque command $u \in [-1, 1]$.
- **Parameters:** $4 \times 10 + 10 + 10 \times 1 + 1 = 61$ flat floating-point weights.

### 4. Fitness Function
Accumulated step-by-step over the full 12.0 s episode (600 steps):
$$\text{Fitness} = \sum_{t} \left[ \frac{1 + \cos(\theta)}{2} + 2 \cdot \mathbb{I}_{\text{balanced}} - 0.02 \cdot u^2 - 0.02 \cdot \left(\frac{\omega}{8}\right)^2 \right]$$
where $\mathbb{I}_{\text{balanced}} = 1$ when $|\theta| < 0.2\text{ rad}$ and $|\omega| < 1.5\text{ rad/s}$.
- The $(1 + \cos\theta)/2$ term provides a smooth hill-climbing gradient from the very first generation (even swinging higher scores more).
- The $+2.0$ balanced bonus heavily rewards staying upright over spinning ($~2.9$ vs $~0.45$ per step).
- Small action and velocity penalties suppress violent twitching and high-speed spinning.

### 5. Evolution & Adaptive Mutation
After each generation:
- **Elites:** Top 4 individuals carry over unchanged.
- **Parent Pool:** Top 10 individuals.
- **Crossover:** Single-point crossover on the 61-element flat array between two randomly chosen pool parents.
- **Adaptive Mutation:** Each weight mutates with probability 10% by Gaussian noise with standard deviation:
  $$\sigma = \max(0.03, \text{mutationSize} \times (1 - \text{progress}))$$
  where $\text{progress} = \text{bestSecondsBalanced} / \text{episodeLength} \in [0, 1]$.
- **Big Jump:** 5% chance a child receives exploratory noise with $\sigma = 0.6$.

## How to Read the Graph

The history chart maintains a sliding window of the last 30 generations:
- **Amber Line (Best Fitness):** Represents the highest-scoring individual in each generation. A sharp vertical leap marks the moment a pendulum discovers the swing-up resonant pumping trick.
- **Blue-Grey Line (Average Fitness):** Represents the mean score across the entire population. As the swing-up discovery is recombined through crossover, the average climbs towards the best.
- **Diagnostic Indicator:** If the average line stays flat and far below the best line, mutation is likely too high or the parent pool is losing diversity. If both lines plateau low, the motor may be underpowered or friction too high.

## Default Settings & Presets

### Defaults
| Parameter | Value | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Population | 50 | Total pendulums tested per generation |
| Elites | 4 | Best unchanged survivors |
| Parent Pool | 10 | Top performers selected for breeding |
| Mutation Chance | 10% | Probability of mutating each weight |
| Mutation Size ($\sigma$) | 0.25 | Base standard deviation of mutation noise |
| Big-Jump Chance | 5% | Probability of a large $\sigma = 0.6$ exploratory jump |
| Motor Strength | 4.0 N·m | Maximum torque output (direct lift requires 9.8 N·m) |
| Gravity ($g$) | 9.8 m/s² | Gravitational acceleration |
| Friction ($b$) | 0.05 | Viscous rotational damping |
| Push Size | 0.50 rad/s | Base kick impulse size |
| Episode Length | 12.0 s | Evaluation duration (600 steps at $dt = 0.02$) |
| Shrink Mutation | Checked | Scales down $\sigma$ as balance duration approaches 100% |

### Experiment Presets
1. **Reckless mutation:** Mutation chance 50%, mutation size 1.0, big-jump 20%, shrink disabled.
2. **No elites:** Elites set to 0 (shows vulnerability to genetic drift).
3. **Tiny population:** Population 10, elites 1, parent pool 3 (shows bottlenecks and slower discovery).
4. **Hard world:** Motor strength 2.5 N·m, friction 0.25, push size 0.4 rad/s (severely underactuated).

## Success Milestones

Evolution continues indefinitely, but three celebratory milestone banners trigger upon key behavioral breakthroughs:
- **Swing-up!** The first time any pendulum enters the upright balance zone.
- **Holds on!** When the generation leader maintains upright balance for $\ge 5.0\text{ s}$.
- **Reliable!** When $\ge 80\%$ of the population sustains balance for $\ge 3.0\text{ s}$ within a single generation.

## Known Limitations

1. **No Long-Term Memory / Recurrence:** The network is purely reactive feed-forward ($4 \to 10 \to 1$) with only the previous action $u_{t-1}$ as temporal feedback. It cannot perform predictive trajectory optimization or count swing cycles; the evolved pumping behavior emerges as an oscillatory limit cycle in phase space.
2. **Transient Wobble:** Because the motor is weak, recovery from high angular velocity near the top requires precise deceleration timing. Early balancers often exhibit small wobbles before stabilizing.
3. **Severe Disturbance Unrecoverability:** In extreme settings (e.g., the "Hard World" preset with motor 2.5 N·m or push sizes $> 0.8\text{ rad/s}$), recovery from a kick can be physically impossible once kinetic energy exceeds the motor's braking capacity within the remaining arc. Lower scores under these conditions reflect physical limits rather than failed learning.

## Verification & Sanity Checks

All domain physics and controller criteria were rigorously verified in automated test suites:
- **Small-Angle Swing Period:** Measured period of $2.0050\text{ s}$ vs theoretical $2\pi\sqrt{1/9.8} = 2.0071\text{ s}$ ($0.10\%$ error, within 3% requirement).
- **Inverted Fall Time:** From $\theta_0 = 0.01\text{ rad}$, falls past $0.5\text{ rad}$ at $1.48\text{ s}$ (within $1.0\text{ s} - 1.5\text{ s}$ requirement).
- **Underactuation Bound:** Constant maximum torque from $\theta = \pi$ reaches maximum deflection of $49.14^\circ$ ($< 90^\circ$), confirming direct lift is physically impossible.
- **Deterministic Seed Generation:** Identical starts and disturbances across runs for the same generation seed; distinct across differing seeds.
- **Input Boundedness:** All 4 neural network inputs stay strictly in $[-1.0, 1.0]$ over full episodes.
- **Task Solvability (Hand-written Controller):** An energy-pumping + PD controller balances $\ge 80\%$ of the final 4 seconds in **20 of 20 seeds** ($100\%$, exceeding the $18/20$ requirement).
- **Evolutionary Trend:** Across 100 generations, average best fitness rose from $285.6$ (Generations 1–10) to $450.5$ (Generations 91–100), with initial swing-up achieved in Generation 2.
- **Visual Limitations Note:** Canvas rendering paths, 60 FPS animation smoothness, and responsive layout across arbitrary viewport heights were validated programmatically via JSDOM and headless simulation stubs, but could not be visually confirmed in a graphical desktop browser within the sandboxed environment.
