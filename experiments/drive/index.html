Reading the graph

The amber line tracks the peak fitness discovered in each generation. Sharp stair-step surges mark breakthroughs, such as learning to slow down for the hairpin apex or clipping the chicane smoothly.

The blue-grey line shows the mean fitness across all 50 cars. When the average climbs toward the best line, the population is converging—successful steering reflexes have replicated across the gene pool.

A large gap indicates exploratory divergence; a narrowing gap means the flock has collectively stabilized its racing line.

Neural Architecture & Evolution

Feed-Forward Topology (102 Weights)

Each car is piloted by an identical 7-input, 10-hidden, 2-output feedforward network evaluated every physics frame:

  • 7 Inputs: 5 distance sensor rays (at -60°, -30°, 0°, +30°, +60° normalized 0..1), normalized speed, and normalized steering angle.
  • 10 Hidden Neurons: Activated with hyperbolic tangent (tanh) to provide non-linear spatial reasoning for apex alignment.
  • 2 Outputs (tanh): Steering torque (-1 full left to +1 full right) and throttle (-1 braking to +1 full acceleration).

Darwinian Selection Without Backpropagation

No gradients or labeled driving lines exist. Top 4 elite genomes survive unchanged. The remaining 46 vehicles are bred from the top 10 parents via single-point crossover with Gaussian mutations. As the best car approaches a full lap, mutation step sizes automatically anneal from 0.25 down toward 0.03, preserving fragile high-speed maneuvers while preventing catastrophic forgetting.
